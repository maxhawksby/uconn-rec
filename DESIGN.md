# UConn Rec: prototype 01

## Design direction

Keep the reference's navy, white, and athletic blue. Use a photo-led invitation to visit the Rec as the main visual moment. Supporting sections stay quiet and practical.

Palette: Husky navy #071C3E, action blue #0755E9, ice blue #ECF2FF, white #FFFFFF, slate #69768C, availability green #16714F.

Type: native system sans (San Francisco on iPhone); heavy, tightly spaced headings, readable regular body copy. Native font scaling stays enabled. Layout: left-aligned content, generous vertical spacing, persistent five-tab navigation. Desktop adds a presentation frame around the actual shared app.

## Feature principles

- Belonging before competition. No rankings or streak pressure.
- One obvious action per flow: start a workout, reserve a spot, share an invite.
- Beginners belong here. Describe intensity, expectations, and options clearly.
- Visibility is a choice. Private workouts and activity sharing off by default.
- Be honest about data. Campus occupancy, profiles, schedules, and photos are samples, not live UConn information.

## Interactive now

Home and sample occupancy, workout exercise selection and editable sets, cardio duration, private workout history, class search/filter/detail/reserve/cancel/waitlist, community local posts/likes, group concepts, profile totals and sharing preference. Data persists locally on each device, without syncing.

## Next design questions

1. Is the home screen primarily for planning a visit or catching up with friends?
2. Should workout logging begin with a reusable routine or a single exercise?
3. How do students discover a group without exposing their location?
4. What should a class cancellation or waitlist promotion feel like?

Group membership, messaging, university login, real reservations, occupancy integrations, and social sharing are not implemented. Production community features need moderation, blocking/reporting, and consent flows.
